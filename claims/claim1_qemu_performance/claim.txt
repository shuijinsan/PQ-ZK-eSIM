Paper: Figure 5, Section 7.2 (Performance and Reliability)

Claim: PQ-ZK-eSIM splits the workload so the eUICC executes no NTT and no
discrete-Gaussian sampling (its online work is 71,680 sparse ternary
additions), while the LPA and Server absorb the heavy lattice computation.
Per-phase timing under QEMU ARM64 emulation confirms this split.

Experiment (QEMU ARM64 emulation):
  qemu-aarch64-static -L /usr/aarch64-linux-gnu \
      artifact/terminal/euicc/build/arm64/bench_pqzkesim_comprehensive --only phase
  (500 authentication trials)

Input: none (parameters are compile-time constants in src/internal/params.h)

Expected output: phase_timing_results.csv
  columns: trial, lpa_precompute_us, euicc_commit_us, challenge_gen_us,
           tee_authtoken_us, euicc_mask_us, lpa_aggregate_us,
           server_verify_us, total_us, euicc_total_us, lpa_total_us,
           tee_total_us, server_total_us

Measured per-phase means (us):
  LPA PreCompute        2046

  Challenge Gen           71
  TEE AuthToken           37
  eUICC Mask/Response   1421

Scope: QEMU ARM64 cryptographic-path timing; biometric capture/matching,
network transit, and NVM commit are excluded.

Analytical eUICC projection (separate model, not a measurement): 4.17 ms
(4.2 ms rounded) dominant-cost projection with W_sec precomputed at 33 MHz
under the stated cycle/AES assumptions.

Runtime: <1 min (QEMU ARM64 emulation)

Resources: 1 CPU core, <100 MB RAM, <10 MB disk

Tolerance:
  - Latency values: faster passes; slower up to +100% (2x) (QEMU/compiler dependent).
  - Deterministic (strict): the eUICC path performs no NTT and no Gaussian
    sampling. This is a code-level property (pqzk_vec_scalar_mul is sparse
    ternary; no NTT function is invoked on the eUICC path), not a timing
    measurement.
