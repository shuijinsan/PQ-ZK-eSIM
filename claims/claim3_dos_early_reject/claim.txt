Paper: Figure 8, Appendix E (Resistance to Real-World Attacks)

Claim: A low-cost MAC pre-verification rejects forged/flooded commitments
before the expensive lattice verification, giving an early-reject speedup.

Experiment (QEMU ARM64 emulation):
  qemu-aarch64-static -L /usr/aarch64-linux-gnu artifact/terminal/euicc/build/arm64/bench_pqzkesim --dos

Input: none

Expected output: dos_results.csv
  columns: test, avg_us
  rows:    MAC_W_Verification, Full_Lattice_Verification, Speedup

Measured:
  MAC_W_Verification         71.00 us
  Full_Lattice_Verification 1110.94 us
  Speedup                     15.6x

Runtime: <1 min

Resources: 1 CPU core, <100 MB RAM, <1 MB disk

Tolerance:
  - Deterministic: Speedup > 1 (MAC is strictly cheaper than full lattice
    verification).
  - timing (avg_us): faster passes; slower up to +100% (2x).

Notes:
  MAC_W_Verification times one epoch of the server sliding-window MAC search:
  HKDF("MAC") followed by AES-256-CMAC over (EID, W_sec, ctr). Full_Lattice_
  Verification times PQC_VerifyEngine on the same request.

The 71.00 us / 1110.94 us / 15.6x figures above are the reference measurement
recorded in this artifact. Absolute wall-clock timings, and therefore the
resulting speedup, depend on the host and on QEMU emulation and will differ on
other machines. The reproduction requirement is that the MAC pre-filter stays
clearly faster than full lattice verification, so any measured speedup well
above 1x is a valid reproduction.
